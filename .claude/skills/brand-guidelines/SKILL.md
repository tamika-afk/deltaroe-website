# Brand Guidelines

This skill keeps everything Crystallume produces visually and verbally consistent.
A brand feels "world-class" not because any single page is brilliant, but because
every page, email, and graphic feels like it came from the same confident company.
Consistency is the cheapest luxury signal there is — use it everywhere.

## How to use this skill

1. Before producing any customer-facing work, read the **Brand Profile** below.
2. If a needed value is marked `[CONFIRM]`, ask the user once, then update this file
   so the answer is captured permanently. Treat this file as living — improve it as
   the brand sharpens.
3. When another skill is doing the actual work (writing, designing, coding), pass it
   the relevant tokens (colors, type, voice rules) rather than restating everything.

## Brand Profile — Crystallume PVD

**Company name:** The official name is **Crystallume PVD** — always write it in full.
It is distinct from the older "Crystallume" name; never refer to the company as just
"Crystallume." (Display/wordmark and copy use "Crystallume PVD".)

**Who they are:** A precision PVD (Physical Vapor Deposition) and DLC (Diamond-Like
Carbon) coating company. They run **new, modern equipment** and have hard-won
process knowledge that solves problems competitors struggle with. Their signature
promise: they **handle customers' parts with the same care and reverence the customer
does** — no damaged, lost, or mishandled work.

**What they sell (positioning pillars):**
- **Capability** — advanced PVD & DLC coatings on modern equipment.
- **Problem-solving expertise** — they fix coating problems others can't.
- **Trust & care** — white-glove handling of irreplaceable, high-value parts.
- **Partnership** — they treat the customer's parts and reputation as their own.

**Primary brand goals:** build awareness and generate qualified leads / new customers
to grow revenue. Every design and copy decision should serve clarity, credibility,
and a clear path to "request a quote / talk to us."

**Audience:** engineers, manufacturing/operations managers, buyers, and shop owners in
aerospace, medical, tooling, automotive, firearms, and high-performance industries.
They are technical, skeptical of hype, and care about results, tolerances, lead time,
and reliability. Write and design for a smart professional, not a casual consumer.

- **Look & feel keywords:** precise, engineered, premium, trustworthy, modern,
  understated. Think high-end instrument or watchmaking — not loud industrial.

### Verbal identity (voice & tone)

- **Voice:** the confident, straight-talking expert who has solved this before. Calm
  authority, not salesy hype. Specific over vague. Respectful of the reader's expertise.
- **Do:** lead with the customer's problem and outcome; use concrete specifics
  (materials, hardness, wear, lead time, tolerances); back claims with evidence.
- **Don't:** use empty superlatives ("the best!"), buzzword soup, or talk down to a
  technical reader. Never overpromise on specs you can't confirm — credibility is the
  whole game in B2B.
- **Tagline (working, confirmed direction 2026-06-08):** "Advanced coatings.
  Uncompromising care." — pairs capability with the trust/handling angle no competitor
  owns. `[Final wording still open to the user's tweak.]`

## When defining a NEW or refreshed brand

If the user wants to evolve the identity:
1. Anchor to the positioning pillars above — visuals should *prove* "precise,
   expert, trustworthy," not just decorate.
2. Present color/type directions as 2–3 distinct concepts with rationale, not 20
   random options. Show them in context (a button, a heading, a coated-part photo).
3. Sample at least one color from the existing logo so nothing clashes.
4. Once chosen, **write the decision back into this file** as the new locked spec.

## Output: a brand one-pager

When asked to "document the brand," produce a concise reference with: logo + clear
space, the locked color tokens (hex + usage), the type scale, imagery do/don't
examples, and the voice rules. Keep it to something a designer or writer can absorb
in two minutes — that's what makes people actually follow it.

## Operating excellence

Hold this work to the standard of a top-tier specialist consultancy.

- **Clarify to elevate.** If the request is ambiguous or missing facts would change the outcome, proceed with the best-judgment default, state the assumption, and end with a short **"To make this better, tell me:"** list.
- **Recommend beyond the ask.** Flag adjacent opportunities or risks in a brief **Recommendations** section — don't silently expand scope.
- **Verify, don't recall.** Check load-bearing claims against the live site, real code, or actual data.
- **Force multipliers:** the live site and real assets — audit against what actually ships, not what the guide claims. If a paid tool or subscription would materially improve the outcome, say so and name what it unlocks.
