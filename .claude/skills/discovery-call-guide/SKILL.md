# Discovery Call Guide

Discovery is where deals are won or wasted. The rep who learns the volume, the current
supplier's price, the pain that actually costs money, and who signs the PO can write a proposal
that lands. The rep who pitches on the first call learns nothing and quotes into the dark. The
trap is treating discovery as a checklist read at the prospect; the questions below are a bank
to choose from, in a conversation that follows the prospect's answers. Done means a call plan,
a filled scorecard, a score with a decision, and the CRM fields the score depends on.

## Inputs
- Who the prospect is (company, contact, title, how they came in: trade show, web inquiry,
  distributor referral, cold), and any prior notes or the trade-show lead record.
- Whether the account is, or should be, served through a distributor. This changes the call:
  the manufacturer qualifies and hands off; it does not quote direct into a distributor's account.
- Public facts checked from the source: what they make, locations, size, industries, machines
  if visible in job postings or press.
- The company's own qualification criteria if they exist (minimum annual potential, target
  industries, materials or applications the products fit). Default minimum potential for a
  pursued deal: state one from the sales manager; if none, use 3 times the median first-year
  value of new accounts won in the last two years.

## Process

### 1. Plan the call
- Pick 8 to 12 questions from the bank, at least one from every section. Order them so the
  easy factual questions come first, pain and decision process in the middle, budget and
  timeline last.
- Write the one hypothesis you want tested ("they are paying a premium for a brand they do not
  need on a roughing operation").

### 2. Question bank
- **Situation and current supplier.** What are you running today, and who supplies it? How long
  with them? What do they do well? What would make you switch, and what has stopped you before?
  Who else has quoted you in the last year?
- **Volumes and spend.** Roughly how many of these do you use a month? How many machines or
  shifts is that across? What is the annual spend on this category, even a range? Is that
  growing, flat, or shrinking, and why?
- **Pain and cost of the problem.** Where does this process cost you time or scrap? What does a
  tool change or a scrapped part cost you in dollars or minutes? How often does it happen?
  What have you already tried? If nothing changes, what happens in twelve months?
- **Technical fit.** Material, operation, machine and spindle, coolant, tolerance, surface finish,
  current parameters. Who sets the parameters, the operator or the programmer?
- **Decision process.** Walk me through how the last supplier change happened. Who was involved?
  Who signs the PO, and at what dollar level does it need a second signature? Is there a trial or
  approval step, and who runs it?
- **Decision criteria.** If two options were close, what decides it: price per piece, cost per
  part, lead time, local stock, technical support, brand?
- **Timeline and trigger.** Is there a date this needs to be solved by? What is driving it, a new
  job, a new machine, a supplier problem?
- **Budget authority.** Is there money set aside, or would this come out of an existing supply
  budget? Who owns that budget?
- **Champion.** Who inside would benefit most if this worked? Would they run a trial for us?
- **Competition.** Who else are you talking to? What did they propose?

### 3. Score MEDDICC
- Seven criteria, each 0 to 3: **M**etrics (0 none, 1 vague, 2 stated, 3 quantified in their
  numbers), **E**conomic buyer (0 unknown, 1 named, 2 met, 3 engaged), **D**ecision criteria
  (0 unknown, 3 written and ours fit), **D**ecision process (0 unknown, 3 mapped with dates),
  **I**dentify pain (0 none, 3 costed and urgent), **C**hampion (0 none, 3 active and testing
  for us), **C**ompetition (0 unknown, 3 known with their weaknesses). Maximum 21.
- Add the potential check: annual potential estimate with method (volume × price, or declared
  spend, or peer median) and whether it clears the minimum.
- Decision: 15 to 21 and potential clears the minimum, **pursue** (opportunity created at
  Discovery or Proposal, next step dated within 7 days). 8 to 14, **qualify further** (a second
  call with the specific gaps as its agenda). 0 to 7 or potential below minimum, **nurture**
  (marketing sequence, revisit in 90 days, no quote).
- Any deal with Economic buyer at 0 or Identify pain at 0 caps at "qualify further" regardless
  of total.

### 4. Route by channel
- If a distributor serves the account or territory, the pursue path is a joint call or a warm
  handoff with the qualification notes, not a direct quote. Record the distributor on the
  opportunity.

### 5. Log it
- The score, each criterion's evidence in one line, potential with method, next step and date,
  distributor if any. These go into the CRM fields; the score is recomputed at each stage.

## Output format

ALWAYS structure as:
1. **Call plan** — the hypothesis, the 8 to 12 chosen questions in order, the one thing to
   avoid doing on this call.
2. **Scorecard** — the seven MEDDICC rows with score, evidence, and the gap that would raise it.
3. **Potential estimate** — number, method, confidence, versus the minimum.
4. **Decision** — pursue, qualify further, or nurture, with the routing (direct, distributor
   joint call, handoff) and the dated next step.
5. **Gaps for the next call** — the questions still unanswered, phrased ready to ask.
6. **CRM log** — the fields to fill, ready to paste.

## Guardrails
- Do not quote or state a price on a discovery call; the answer to "what does it cost" is a
  question about their volume and current cost per part.
- Scores use only what the prospect actually said or what was verified; an assumed economic
  buyer scores 0, not 1.
- Never disparage the incumbent supplier; ask what they do well first.
- Distributor-served accounts route through the distributor. Never coach a rep to bypass the
  channel because the prospect asked for a direct price.
- Personal data collected on the call (mobile numbers, personal emails) goes into the CRM only.

## Operating excellence

Hold this work to the standard of a top-tier specialist consultancy.

- **Clarify to elevate.** If the request is ambiguous or missing facts would change the outcome, proceed with the best-judgment default, state the assumption, and end with a short **"To make this better, tell me:"** list.
- **Recommend beyond the ask.** Flag adjacent opportunities or risks in a brief **Recommendations** section — don't silently expand scope.
- **Verify, don't recall.** Check load-bearing claims against the live system, real code, or actual data.
- **Force multipliers:** Gong or Chorus (paid) call recording with question-tracking so scores come from what was said, not what was remembered; ZoomInfo or Apollo (paid) for the org chart behind the economic-buyer question; the company's application or speeds-and-feeds engine to turn stated parameters into a cost-per-part estimate on the second call. If a paid tool or subscription would materially improve the outcome, say so and name what it unlocks.
