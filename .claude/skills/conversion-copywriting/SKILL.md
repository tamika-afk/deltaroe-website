# Conversion Copywriting (B2B, technical)

Crystallume's buyers are engineers, buyers, and shop owners — technical, skeptical, and
allergic to hype. The copy's job is to be **clear, credible, and specific**, lead with
their problem, prove competence, and make the next step obvious. Persuasion here comes
from precision and proof, not adjectives.

Write in the brand voice defined in [[brand-guidelines]]: confident expert, calm
authority, specific over vague, never salesy.

## Core principles

1. **Lead with the customer's problem and outcome, not your company.** Open with what
   they're trying to achieve or fix (a part that wears out, a coating that failed, a
   supplier who damaged their work), then position Crystallume as the answer. "You" before
   "we."
2. **Be specific. Specifics are credibility.** Name materials, coating types (PVD, DLC),
   measurable benefits (hardness, wear life, friction reduction, temperature
   resistance), industries, and process details. Vague claims ("high quality,
   industry-leading") are invisible to a technical reader; concrete facts land.
3. **Prove every claim.** Pair assertions with evidence — specs, certifications,
   examples, before/after, testimonials. In B2B, an unproven claim is worse than no
   claim because it costs trust.
4. **Sell the differentiators.** Crystallume's edges (from [[brand-guidelines]]): modern
   equipment, problem-solving expertise others lack, and white-glove care for the
   customer's parts. Make these vivid and concrete — especially the care promise, which
   is emotionally resonant for someone shipping irreplaceable parts.
5. **One clear next step.** Every page drives toward "Request a Quote" / "Talk to us."
   CTAs should be specific and low-friction ("Get a coating quote," not "Submit").
6. **Respect the reader's time.** Front-load the point. Short sentences. Scannable
   structure — strong headings, tight bullets. They skim before they commit.

## Structure patterns

**Hero section:**
- Headline: the core value proposition — what + for whom + the edge. Clear beats clever.
- Subhead: one sentence expanding it with a concrete benefit or differentiator.
- Primary CTA + a credibility cue (industries served, certification, years of expertise).

**Service page (PVD / DLC):**
- What it is and what problem it solves (plain, then technical).
- Benefits tied to outcomes (wear, friction, hardness, appearance, lead time).
- Materials/parts/applications it suits.
- Proof (specs, cases, certs).
- Why Crystallume for this specifically (equipment, expertise, care).
- CTA.

**Why-us / about:** tell the competence-and-care story with specifics and evidence, not
self-congratulation. Show the equipment, the process, the standards, the handling.

**FAQ:** answer the real objections — "Can you coat my material?", "What's the lead
time?", "How do you protect my parts?", "What if it's a difficult/unusual job?" Honest,
specific answers reduce friction and rank well in search (see [[seo-optimization]]).

## Headlines

Write 5–10 options, ranging from clear/literal to more evocative, then recommend one
with a one-line rationale. Avoid empty superlatives. A good B2B headline says something
true and specific that a competitor couldn't truthfully copy.

## Editing existing copy

When improving text: cut hype and filler, replace vague claims with specifics, lead
with the reader's interest, tighten sentences, and add a clear CTA. Show the before/after
and briefly note what changed and why, so the user learns the pattern.

## Quality bar

Would a skeptical engineer find this credible and clear? Is every claim either provable
or removed? Is the next step obvious? Does it sound like a confident expert, not a
brochure? If not, revise.

Coordinate with [[seo-optimization]] so primary pages naturally include the terms
buyers actually search — without ever sacrificing readability for keywords.

## Operating excellence

Hold this work to the standard of a top-tier specialist consultancy.

- **Clarify to elevate.** If the request is ambiguous or missing facts would change the outcome, proceed with the best-judgment default, state the assumption, and end with a short **"To make this better, tell me:"** list.
- **Recommend beyond the ask.** Flag adjacent opportunities or risks in a brief **Recommendations** section — don't silently expand scope.
- **Verify, don't recall.** Check load-bearing claims against the live site, real code, or actual data.
- **Force multipliers:** voice-of-customer sources (reviews, sales calls, chat logs) — ask for them; copy built on real customer language converts better than invented phrasing. If a paid tool or subscription would materially improve the outcome, say so and name what it unlocks.
